# Radar-inspired scalar event demo: design references

Researched 2026-10-12. This is a bounded design recommendation for phase 4 of the [map improvements proposal](../superpowers/plans/2026-10-12-observation-map-improvements-proposal.md), not a measured-emitter benchmark or implementation approval. Only this note was created; no dataset was downloaded or adopted.

## Representation and evidence boundary

Keep the existing [event contract](../../spec/agility.ts) and [stage-0 semantics](../agility-stage0.md): one already separated emitter/candidate stream, event arrival timestamps, and supplied scalar Frequency, preceding-event PRI and PW. A/B identity is supplied synthetic provenance. Similarity must never assign it. Each capture has independent identity even when its generating schedule is shared.

Frequency means a carrier/centre-frequency descriptor per pulse. A schedule of carrier changes between events is representable; an intra-pulse frequency sweep is not. MathWorks explicitly models a carrier hop as a shifted waveform, while its example also has a separate linear-FM sweep bandwidth. Those are different quantities; the demo must not call its event-level staircase a chirp. [MathWorks frequency-agility example](https://www.mathworks.com/help/radar/ug/frequency-agility-in-radar-communcations-ew-systems.html).

## Primary technical support

| Behaviour | What the source supports | Boundary for this demo |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier hopping | A predefined schedule can change transmitted carrier frequency; the worked example uses 10 GHz, a 500 kHz hop, PRF 4 kHz and PW 100 us. | These are worked-example values, not a typical-emitter distribution. Our hop order and multi-channel coordination are invented. [MathWorks](https://www.mathworks.com/help/radar/ug/frequency-agility-in-radar-communcations-ew-systems.html). |
| Staggered PRI | A nonconstant vector of PRFs implements staggering; the example alternates 1 and 2 kHz with PW 100 us. Their reciprocal intervals are 1000 and 500 us. | Supports discrete interval changes, not our exact F/PRI/PW pairing. [MathWorks staggered PRFs](https://www.mathworks.com/help/phased/ug/waveforms-with-staggered-prfs.html). |
| Jitter and dwell/switch | Tang et al. describe jitter as random variation about a mean, commonly Gaussian or uniform and below 30%; dwell/switch holds fixed PRI levels for multiple pulses then switches through levels. | A seeded bounded jitter or a held-level sequence is defensible as an illustrative family; no universal percentage or dwell count follows. [Tang et al., §1.1](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/joe.2019.0487). |
| PW and RF changes | Qin et al. study inter-pulse PRI, PW and RF modulation, including stagger/jitter/dwell forms, and construct synthetic work states from those channels. | Supports considering multiple changing descriptors. It does not validate our specific joint schedule, emitter attribution or matcher. [Qin et al., §2.1 and §5.1](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/rsn2.12609). |
| Observation imperfections | Tang et al. explain that missed pulses accumulate intervening intervals into a larger measured PRI; spurious pulses split intervals. Qin et al. explicitly separate measurement error, lost pulses and spurious pulses in simulation. | Missing scalar measurements, dropped events, intentional timing jitter and measurement error must remain separate controls. Do not copy Tang's replacement/repair algorithm into originals. [Tang et al., §2.1](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/joe.2019.0487), [Qin et al., §5.4](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/rsn2.12609). |

## Recommended synthetic choices

These are **our engineering choices**, using the worked examples above only to anchor scale. They are not source-derived joint operating modes or estimates of field hardware.

- Store carrier in MHz around 10000, with a four-level palette `10000, 10000.5, 10001, 10001.5`; use PRI levels within 250–1000 us and PW levels within 40–100 us. The 0.5 MHz spacing and 10 GHz centre follow one example; four levels, extended total span and smaller PW values are our choices. Keep every PW below the shortest local interval so pulses do not overlap; this is a conservative construction constraint, not a claim that all radar descriptors obey our timing convention. [MathWorks duty-cycle definition](https://www.mathworks.com/help/signal/ref/dutycycle.html).
- Shared A/B mode: one finite 64-event stepped carrier sequence with discrete staggered PRI and varying PW tied to the same event-level state. Make separate captures from the same template; this illustrates shared behaviour across supplied profiles.
- A distinctive mode: 64 events with held carrier/PRI/PW states, e.g. eight events per state, and a different state order. Name it a synthetic coordinated dwell schedule; its dwell length and cross-channel coupling are invented.
- B distinctive mode: 64 events with a fixed seeded irregular hop order, intentional PRI jitter about e.g. 600 us bounded at ±10%, and a seeded varying PW schedule. Its clean/noisy pair should retain the same underlying realisation of intentional jitter. Independently drawing an entirely new random schedule need not produce the same shape, even if both belong to the jitter family.
- Eight recordings suffice: shared A/B (2), A clean/noisy (2), B clean/noisy (2), B shared→distinctive transition (128 events), incomplete C (64 events). Counts, transition location and independent capture labels are construction decisions. All channels should vary sufficiently to test coordination rather than constant-channel matching paths. C can retain Frequency/PRI while PW is entirely null, leaving the full three-channel evidence gate unsatisfied without pretending it contradicts A/B.
- Begin with clean modes. For the noisy companion, a bounded zero-mean scalar perturbation such as frequency ±0.01 MHz, PRI ±1 us and PW ±0.2 us is a small readable initial control. These numbers are deliberately chosen, not calibrated receiver accuracy or an SNR model. Document seed, PRNG, distribution, units and amplitudes; verify actual matcher results before accepting the presentation. Do not tune the matcher to force the story.

## Arrival time, PRI and gaps

For consecutive synthetic events, set `t[0] = 0`, `PRI[0] = null` unless explicitly supplying prior capture context, and construct `t[i] = t[i-1] + PRI[i]` for `i > 0`. This is the application's preceding-event convention: the interval belongs to the event ending it. Generate the timing schedule first, then Frequency/PW at each event; never shift channels separately. If scalar PRI perturbation is used as the interval schedule, accumulate that same positive value into time and call it a simulated interval estimate/schedule. Do not claim independent ToA/PRI receiver errors from this construction.

For **missing channel measurements**, retain the timestamped event and write null; neither timing nor the other measurements move. For a **dropped detection**, remove the event without closing the time gap. If the demo claims observed preceding-detected-event PRI, supply the sum of the omitted intervals at the next retained event (equivalently its observed ToA difference); do not keep the last transmitter interval and label it the observed gap. If supplied upstream PRI instead retains the transmitter interval, provenance must explicitly say so and preserve the visible discrepancy. The simplest first demo can use null measurements for C and reserve dropped-event examples for focused generator tests. Any partial-window first PRI remains original capture evidence, not a new interval inferred from the window boundary.

The formula/convention above comes from our existing contract. Tang et al.'s missed-pulse explanation supports the summed-gap consequence, not repairing originals. Do not interpolate missing evidence or infer omitted pulses in the application. Validate finite strictly increasing timestamps, positive present PRI/PW, exact window identity, deterministic regeneration and the intended whole/partial/group outcomes using the existing matcher.

## Turing release recheck: reference only

The official [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/alan-turing-institute/turing-synthetic-radar-dataset/blob/main/README.md) identifies purely simulated deinterleaving PDWs and lists ToA (us), centre frequency (MHz), PW (us), AoA and amplitude; **PRI is not a released PDW field**. Its metadata declares Apache-2.0. The [current release tree](https://huggingface.co/datasets/alan-turing-institute/turing-synthetic-radar-dataset/tree/main) exposes `scan`, `stare` and `archive` directories plus the card; only metadata was inspected. Institutional provenance is corroborated by the [Turing publication page](https://www.turing.ac.uk/news/publications/turing-synthetic-radar-dataset-dataset-pulse-deinterleaving) and its linked [authors' paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03856v1).

The card's approximately four-billion-pulse/up-to-90-emitter summary differs from the publication's nearly-three-billion/up-to-110 summary. Treat these as release/documentation differences, not resolved measurements; do not use a headline count as our validation. The paper describes receiver tuning causing lost detections and publishes broad marginal Frequency/PW statistics, which do not establish joint operating modes. Its article license is separate from the dataset card's license. No assets are incorporated, so no redistribution/license adoption is proposed. Any later import would first need a pinned revision, resolved release statistics, actual artifact/license checks, emitter-stream extraction and explicit missing-detection-aware PRI semantics.

No transmitter, propagation, receiver, IQ, deinterleaving or identification simulation is implied by this tiny demo. It tests and explains this application's scalar evidence/matching model; measured-emitter accuracy and realistic hardware noise remain unverified.
